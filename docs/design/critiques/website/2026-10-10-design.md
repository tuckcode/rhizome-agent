## The slogan, verbatim

The words on the page are **"Your work. Your memory."** I found it in five places:
1. Set into the hero image `assets/hero.png`, under the "rhizome AGENT" wordmark. The image also contains the line "Chat with Prime. Keep what matters."
2. The hero image alt text: "Rhizome Agent. Your work. Your memory. Chat with Prime. Keep what matters."
3. The bold heading of the aside next to the H1 ("Your work. Your memory.").
4. `og:description`: "A harness that doesn't forget. Your work. Your memory."
5. The brand pack: `docs/design/brand/2026-09-13/rhizome-ascii.txt`, the README, and the image-generation prompt in `PROMPTS.md`.

**Where it came from:** it entered through the 2026-09-13 image-generation prompts and the art they produced. No copy decision for it appears anywhere in the docs.

**The actual H1** is "A harness that doesn't forget." It's the second headline on the page. On a 1440×900 screen it starts at about y 790, below the hero image, so it's below the fold.

## Blockers

**WS-B1 · Copy / positioning · The page describes the old architecture.**
- It says "A chat app built around Prime Agent".
- The fact strip reads "HARNESS · Prime Agent", "TOOLS FROM · Claude Code", "TOOLS FROM · Hermes Agent".
- Other lines: "Bring your own model through Prime", "Prime Agent runs the session: tools, skills, and provider login", "./install.sh fetches Prime Agent for chat".
- ADR-0180 (2026-10-09) says Rhizome owns its loop and Prime and Hermes are optional engines. The app already has its own `native_chat_start/send/approval_reply` commands.
- The doctrine also says Hermes only lends *contracts* (approval, observability), not tools, so "Tools from Hermes Agent" overstates it.

*Fix:* rewrite around the Rhizome loop and the vault (see Positioning). Mention Prime and Hermes once, in a "What runs where" section, as "optional engines". If the native loop isn't in the developer-preview build yet, say so plainly: "Chat runs on Prime Agent today; Rhizome's own loop is landing in the preview."

**WS-B2 · Copy / visual design · An unapproved slogan is baked into the hero art.** "Your work. Your memory." can't be edited, translated or tested without regenerating the PNG. It also makes the hero image the only heading on the first screen, and it's generic: any notes app or AI memory product could say it. *Fix:* remove all text from the hero image (or drop the image; see WS-B3). Delete the slogan from the alt text, the aside and `og:description`. Put the chosen headline in real HTML text.

**WS-B3 · Layout / visual design · The first screen is a glossy 3D render, not the product.**
- At 1440×900, `hero.png` (1440×702) fills the first screen with a glossy green molecule over root tendrils. That's the generic AI-art look the brief wants to avoid, and nothing in it shows the product.
- The real H1, the description and both buttons are pushed below the fold.
- At 390 wide, the baked line "Chat with Prime. Keep what matters." renders at about 7px and can't be read.

*Fix:* make the hero real HTML text plus one product-truth visual (see the mock in `hero-mock/`). At most, keep the six-node mark at icon size in the nav.

## Worth fixing

**WS-W1 · Color · Secondary text fails contrast (37 elements).** #70716B on #070D0A is 3.98:1 (axe `color-contrast`). It covers the eyebrows, the aside, the fact-strip labels, the FIG captions and the footer. *Fix:* use DS `--fg-tertiary` #929C86 (6.84:1 on this background, 6.59:1 on `--bg`) for secondary text, and `--fg-secondary` #ADB6A3 for body copy.

**WS-W2 · Visual design · The page doesn't use the app's palette or type.**
- Fonts are Geist and Geist Mono; the background is #070D0A and the text is cream #F1ECE2.
- The design system is Inter plus JetBrains Mono, with `--bg` #0E120C and `--fg` #E6EBE0.
- The cream text and paper-tone hero drift toward anti-pattern 9. The site and the app look like two brands.

*Fix:* import `colors_and_type.css` and add a marketing type scale on top (e.g. 48/34/20/16/13). Keep mono for kickers and paths only.

**WS-W3 · Hierarchy · "What it does" lists features but never shows the loop.**
- The four cards (Chat with Prime, A vault you own, Notes beside the chat, Research and links) each have three numbered labels (1.1–4.3) that add no information.
- The FIG drawings are generic: FIG.04 is a six-spoke star, FIG.03 a wireframe.
- What's actually distinctive is chat → tools → promote → recall (`memory-loop.md`), and it never appears.

*Fix:* replace the grid with one "The loop" section: four steps, each with a real artifact (see Positioning §4).

**WS-W4 · Interaction · The demo is a black box.**
- `demo.mp4` has no poster, so it shows a near-black frame with a small logo.
- It has no captions or text alternative (axe flags `video-caption` for review).
- "Twenty seconds. The whole loop." doesn't say what happens in it.

*Fix:* use a poster frame taken from the moment the promoted note appears, add a WebVTT caption track, and write three lines under the video naming each step.

**WS-W5 · Copy · Some claims aren't backed by the app or the docs.**
- "MODELS · DeepSeek and more": the app's provider catalog lists Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter, Ollama, LM Studio and a custom provider. DeepSeek isn't named.
- "memory, wiki, and second brain" is three buzzwords for one folder.
- "No lock-in" is an assertion with nothing to show it.

*Fix:* list the catalog providers ("Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter, local via Ollama or LM Studio, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint"). Replace the buzzwords with what the folder does ("Markdown files with wikilinks, versioned in git").

**WS-W6 · Hierarchy · Too many primary buttons.** "GITHUB ↗" in the nav and "VIEW ON GITHUB ↗" are both solid cream, next to "WATCH THE DEMO". That's anti-pattern 7, and all three are uppercase mono. *Fix:* one solid primary ("Get started", to #run or the start docs), the demo as a text link, and GitHub as a plain nav link. Use sentence case.

**WS-W7 · Accessibility · Landmarks and links.** There's no `<main>` (axe `landmark-one-main`; 18 `region` hits). The "Tolaria" link in the footer is 1.59:1 against the surrounding text with no underline (`link-in-text-block`). Headings read "01Chat with Prime" because the number has no space. *Fix:* wrap the content in `<main>`, underline inline links, and hide the number from the heading's accessible name or add a space.

**WS-W8 · Layout · Mobile at 390 wide.**
- The section links (Features, Demo, Run it) are hidden with no menu, so only GitHub is left.
- The `git clone` URL breaks mid-word across three lines.
- The page is 4,961px tall, and the hero image eats the first 280px with text nobody can read.

*Fix:* keep the anchors as a horizontally scrolling row, put commands in a code block that scrolls sideways (`white-space: pre; overflow-x: auto`), and use the text hero.

**WS-W9 · Copy · Two-part fragment headings that don't say anything.** "Chat that remembers. Notes that stay yours.", "Twenty seconds. The whole loop." and "Three commands. Developer preview." are cadence, not content, and the second half is greyed out. *Fix:* write headings that state the fact: "How a session becomes a note", "Watch one loop (0:20)", "Run it from source (macOS)".

## Minor

- **WS-M1:** `og:image` and `og:description` carry the slogan. *Fix:* regenerate them from the new hero.
- **WS-M2:** there are 9 font sizes (11, 11.5, 12, 12.5, 14, 18, 22, 42, 72px). *Fix:* use 5 steps.
- **WS-M3:** FIG.01–05 labels and the 1.1–4.3 numbering are pseudo-technical decoration. *Fix:* keep a FIG label only where a caption explains the figure.
- **WS-M4:** in the footer, "Prime Agent ↗" sits under the TRUST heading. *Fix:* move it under Project, or a "Built with" group.
- **WS-M5:** focus is the browser's default 1px outline. It's visible, but it isn't the design system's style. *Fix:* `:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--accent); outline-offset: 2px }`.
- **WS-M6:** the hero image `<img>` has the slogan as alt text and the logo has an empty alt. *Fix:* once the hero is text, give the decorative art empty alt.
- **WS-M7:** the requirements line puts "Node `^20.19.0` or `>=22.12.0`" before what the app is. *Fix:* lead with "macOS, build from source, about 10 minutes", then a collapsible requirements list.

Things that pass: no horizontal overflow at any width, `lang="en"` is set, every link is reachable by Tab in a logical order, the native video controls work by keyboard, and nothing animates under reduced motion.

## Positioning and story

### 1. Who lands here and what they need to decide

A developer who already uses Claude Code, Hermes or a similar terminal agent clicks through from GitHub or a post. Within about 10 seconds they need to decide: is this a different kind of harness, one whose memory I can read and own, worth building from source on my Mac?

### 2. What the page claims vs. what's actually distinctive

| The page says | What the docs say is distinctive |
|---|---|
| "A chat app built around Prime Agent" | **Its own harness and loop** (ADR-0180). Prime and Hermes are optional engines, and the app degrades to the bare Rhizome loop if one fails. |
| "a vault that works as your memory, wiki, and second brain" | **The vault is the single source of truth for memory** ("Vault wins"). No second store, no `MEMORY.md`, no silent dual-write. |
| "Save a reply as a note" (bullet 3.3) | **Promote is an explicit, reviewable step**: "proposal → diff → approve → vault write", never "auto-wiki from every turn" (`memory-loop.md`, doctrine). This is the most defensible idea and it gets one bullet. |
| "Agent reads it through MCP" | **Recall with provenance**: the next session searches the vault, and answers show "From your vault" with the notes they used (#25, shipped). |
| "Git-friendly by design" | **Memory you can diff and revert**: the vault is a git repo, with Changes and History views and commits in the app. |
| "Notes beside the chat" | **Chat-first**: the note opens beside the conversation and never swallows it (DESIGN anti-pattern 4). |
| (missing) | **Work you can watch**: every tool call is a structured card in the thread, observable and interruptible (the Hermes contract; DESIGN anti-pattern 3). |
| (missing) | **The problem it solves** (vision doc): coding agents are good at doing work but bad at lasting knowledge; notes apps have structure but weak agents. Rhizome joins the two. |
| "Research and links" | Research and Distill ingest sources into notes. The graph and Mycelium show what the vault and the agent runs touched. These are supporting features, not the headline. |

### 3. Candidate headlines and subheads

Precise, concrete, sentence case, no hype. None of them needs a metric.

1. **Every session leaves notes, not just a transcript.**
   *Sub:* Rhizome Agent is a desktop agent harness. You chat, the agent works through tools you can see, and what's worth keeping is promoted into Markdown notes in a local, git-backed vault. The next session searches those notes first.
   *Why:* states the outcome that is unique (the vision's "every important outcome lands as inspectable knowledge… not only a chat log") and contrasts it with what every other agent leaves behind.
2. **An agent whose memory you can diff.**
   *Sub:* What the agent keeps is a Markdown change in your git vault. You see the diff, you approve it, and you can revert it like code.
   *Why:* the most distinctive mechanism (proposal → diff → approve), in developer language, and the most memorable of the eight.
3. **The agent's memory is a folder you can open.**
   *Sub:* No hidden memory file and no cloud store. Rhizome keeps what matters as plain Markdown with wikilinks, on your disk, in git.
   *Why:* "Vault wins" and "no second store", made physical. Answers "where does my data go" before anyone asks.
4. **Chat with an agent. Keep what it learned.**
   *Sub:* A desktop harness with its own agent loop, structured tool cards, and a local Markdown vault that the next session reads from.
   *Why:* the plainest version. It reads like the fixed slogan, but says what's actually kept.
5. **A harness for agents that should remember your project.**
   *Sub:* Decisions, research and procedures become notes in your vault, linked and versioned, and answers cite the notes they used.
   *Why:* aimed at project continuity, the job a coding developer feels, and brings in provenance.
6. **Agent work in the thread. Knowledge in the vault.**
   *Sub:* Every tool call is a card you can follow and stop. Every result worth keeping becomes a note you can edit.
   *Why:* names both halves of the product and the split between them (operational vs durable memory, per the doctrine).
7. **Your notes are the agent's long-term memory.**
   *Sub:* Rhizome Agent searches your Markdown vault before it answers and writes back only what you promote.
   *Why:* reverses the usual direction (the agent serves the notes), and says "only what you promote" up front.
8. **Built for agents that work for weeks, not one chat.**
   *Sub:* Sessions come and go. The vault stays, with plain files, wikilinks and git history the next session builds on.
   *Why:* echoes the vision's "long-running agents" positioning. Use it only if long-run jobs are in the preview build.

**My top 3:** #1 for the hero, with #2 as the "The vault" section heading and #3 as the meta description.

### 4. Proposed page outline

1. **Nav:** wordmark (six-node mark at 18px) · How it works · The vault · Run it · GitHub ↗. Sentence case, one button style.
2. **Hero:**
   - Kicker: "Developer preview · macOS · open source".
   - H1 #1 and its subhead.
   - One primary button "Get started" and a text link "Watch the 20-second loop".
   - Three facts in mono: Memory = Markdown + git · Models = bring your own · License = AGPL-3.0.
   - Visual: the loop figure (see `hero-mock/`).
3. **The problem (2 sentences):** "Coding agents do the work and forget it. Notes apps remember but can't do the work." Then the one line that bridges them. Source: the vision doc's Problem section.
4. **How a session becomes a note:** four steps, each with a real artifact.
   - ① Chat: the composer.
   - ② Work: tool cards in the thread (search_notes · 3 notes, get_note).
   - ③ Promote: the diff you approve.
   - ④ Recall: the next session's answer with "From your vault".
5. **The vault (headline #2):** a tree of real paths, a commit list from History, and the graph built from a real vault. One paragraph: plain Markdown, wikilinks, git, open in any editor.
6. **Work you can watch:** a transcript crop with tool cards, a pending or approval state and the Stop button. Copy: observable, interruptible, approval fails closed (doctrine contracts, stated as behaviour).
7. **What runs where:** an architecture diagram. Rhizome loop → tools and MCP → vault on disk. Models from the providers you choose. Optional engines: Prime Agent, Hermes. One line: "If an optional engine fails, chat falls back to the Rhizome loop."
8. **Demo:** the video with a poster, captions, and a three-line text description.
9. **Run it:** status first ("no signed build yet, build from source"), three commands in a code block that scrolls sideways, requirements in a disclosure, and links to Getting started and Agent instructions.
10. **Footer:** project links, license, and lineage (Tolaria, Mindwalk credit). Prime Agent under "Optional engines".

### 5. Visual ideas without AI clichés

All drawn in the Mycelium palette: `--bg` #0E120C, `--fg-tertiary` lines, one `--accent` mint highlight per figure. No gradients, sparkles, robots, glows or 3D renders.

1. **A vault grown from real notes.** A flat 2D SVG network built from the project's own docs vault (memory-loop, harness-doctrine, promote-vs-distill…). Real titles in mono labels, edges in `--border-strong`. Only the newest promoted note is mint, with its two new links. It can slowly add one node on scroll, with no motion under reduced motion. It also replaces the 3D molecule as the brand image: the mark becomes the actual graph shape.
2. **Thread ↔ note, side by side.** The transcript with structured tool cards on the left. The Markdown file it wrote on the right, with frontmatter (`session: sess_7f2a`) and highlighted `[[wikilinks]]`. A thin mint connector from the "promote" card to the file name. This is the hero mock.
3. **Memory as a git diff.** A History-style commit list ("promote: promote-vs-distill · sess_7f2a") over a unified diff with `+` lines in `--ok`, captioned "Reviewed like code. Revertible like code." It shows ownership and control without claiming them.
4. **Three sessions on one root line.** A horizontal timeline (a literal rhizome stem) with sessions 1–3 as nodes. Sessions 1 and 2 each drop a note below the line. Session 3's answer carries a "From your vault" chip pointing back to those notes. It shows recall over time with no screenshot needed.
